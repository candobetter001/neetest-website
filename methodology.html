Methodology

No black box.

Every number the dashboard shows you is derived from a formula you can verify here. If the tool tells you that Endocrinology is your weakest topic, you should be able to check the math.

1. Accuracy

The simplest one. For any subject, topic, or attempt:

accuracy = correct ÷ attempted

Skipped questions don’t count as either correct or attempted. Marked-for-review questions count as attempted only if a final option was selected.

2. Time-per-question

Average seconds spent on each question that was attempted:

avg_time = total_seconds_on_attempted_qs ÷ attempted

If you switched tabs or your screen locked, that idle time is excluded — we only count time when the question was actually on screen.

3. Weakness score (the most important one)

This is the formula that ranks your weak areas on the dashboard. For any topic:

weakness = (1 − accuracy) × √(avg_time / median_time) × topic_weight

In plain English, it’s the product of three factors:

  1. (1 − accuracy) — how often you get it wrong. If you’re at 60% accuracy, this term is 0.40.
  2. √(avg_time / median_time) — how much slower than typical you are on this topic. Median time is computed across all NEETest users on the same topic. Square root softens the penalty so a topic that takes 1.5× longer isn’t treated as 1.5× weaker — it’s √1.5 ≈ 1.22× weaker.
  3. topic_weight — how often that topic appears in real NEET PG and INI-CET papers. Endocrinology has a higher weight than, say, History of Medicine.
Why this matters: a topic where you’re wrong half the time but it appears once a year is less urgent than a topic where you’re wrong 30% of the time but it appears on every paper. The formula encodes that judgement instead of just sorting by accuracy.

4. Topic weights

We compute topic weights from the last 5 years of released NEET PG and INI-CET papers. For each topic we count how many questions referenced it, then normalise so weights sum to 1.0 within each subject.

The weights are recomputed once a year after the new papers are released and added to our bank. The current snapshot is from NEET PG 2024 + INI-CET 2024.

5. Mock test percentile

For each full-length mock you attempt:

percentile = (number_of_users_you_outscored ÷ total_users) × 100

We use a minimum sample size of 200 prior attempts before reporting a percentile. If fewer than 200 users have attempted that specific mock, we report your raw score and an asterisked “percentile pending” instead.

6. Day-over-day trend

The line you see on the dashboard sparkline is a 7-day exponentially-weighted moving average of your daily accuracy. It smooths out single bad days so the trend reflects real improvement, not noise.

EWMAtoday = 0.3 × accuracytoday + 0.7 × EWMAyesterday

7. What we deliberately do not measure

A few things commercial platforms measure that we don’t, on purpose:

  • Streak. Streaks reward showing up, not learning. You can “keep a streak alive” by attempting 5 easy questions a day and stagnate for a year. We don’t show one.
  • Leaderboards. The exam isn’t a leaderboard. Your competition is the cutoff, not other aspirants.
  • “Confidence score.” No prep tool can measure how confident you actually are on exam day. We don’t invent metrics that sound good but mean nothing.

8. Where this is implemented

The formulas above are implemented in the Android app’s analytics layer and in the website’s dashboard JavaScript. If you find a discrepancy between what the tool shows you and what the formulas predict, that’s a bug — please email candobetter001@gmail.com with your account ID and we’ll fix it.

9. Will the formulas change?

Yes, occasionally. If we tune a weight or update topic weights after a new exam year, that’s logged on the changelog with the date and the reason. We don’t silently change scoring — you should be able to see why a topic’s rank moved.
