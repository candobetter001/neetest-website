Acute vs Chronic Inflammation

Robbins, Ch. 3 · ~5 min read

Key differences

  • Onset: Acute = fast (minutes-hours), Chronic = slow (days-weeks)
  • Cellular infiltrate: Acute = neutrophils, Chronic = lymphocytes + macrophages + plasma cells
  • Tissue injury: Acute = mild & self-limited, Chronic = often progressive
  • Fibrosis: Acute = absent, Chronic = present

Causes of chronic inflammation

  • Persistent infection (TB, leprosy, syphilis, hepatitis virus)
  • Hypersensitivity disease (autoimmune, asthma)
  • Prolonged exposure to toxic agents (silica → silicosis)

Mnemonic — "GRANULOMA"

Giant cells · Rim of lymphocytes · Amoeba (some causes) · Necrosis (caseating in TB) · Unspecific irritants · Leprosy · Osteomyelitis (chronic) · Metabolic · Aspergillus
